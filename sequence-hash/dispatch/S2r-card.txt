FIX 1 — SequenceMAC key derivation is BETTER than HMAC; Hop 1 is wrong.
The spec (SequenceFunction, lines 245-260) computes the derived key block
K' = Derive(K,H,b) ONCE, then processes it from DISTINCT "run-up" states: the
inner cascade absorbs `HeaderI(b,F,K) || K'`, the outer absorbs
`HeaderO(b,F,S,K) || S' || K'`. HeaderI and HeaderO are guaranteed distinct
(leading 8 bytes 'SEQHSH_I' vs 'SEQHSH_O', line 237). Hence the inner key k_I and
outer key k_O are the keyed compression cascade evaluated at TWO DISTINCT
header-prefixed inputs.
  - Unlike HMAC (inner/outer keys = K⊕ipad, K⊕opad — RELATED keys, needing a
    PRF-under-related-key/RKA assumption, Gaži §1.2), SequenceMAC's k_I,k_O are
    INDEPENDENT under ORDINARY (data-input) PRF security of the compression f.
    The spec designed this deliberately (line 218: "independently-keyed inner and
    outer hash functions").
  - So Hop 1 must NOT invoke the HMAC→NMAC related-key bridge. Replace it with a
    clean domain-separation/independence argument: replacing (k_I,k_O) by
    independent uniform values is a STANDARD PRF/URF hop on the two distinct-input
    run-up evaluations of f — a normal PRF-advantage term, NO related-key
    assumption. Redefine ε_KS accordingly (the compression-PRF cost of the
    domain-separated key-derivation run-up), and fold in the Derive(K) hash call
    when |K|>b. State the assumption as ordinary PRF security of f, not RKA.
