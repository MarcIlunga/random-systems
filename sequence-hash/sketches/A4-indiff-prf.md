# A4 — SequenceMAC PRF security from hash indifferentiability (R3)

> **Superseded integration warning (2026-07-20).** The typed
> `MetricResourceAlgebra` / `ProtocolIndifferentiable` / `attachQ` / `edistQ`
> route and its cited RS-carrier receipts below are a historical,
> non-operative snapshot: those APIs were deleted and the old carrier modules
> are gated. The mathematical R3 sketch is preserved, but it must be re-audited
> against the flat `AbstractCrypto` API and the current RS carrier before reuse;
> this document is not evidence of a completed bridge.

## Result and scope

R3 is the hash-agnostic composition theorem.  It does not inspect a
Merkle--Damgård compression function.  Its two legs are

$$
H \approx_{\mathrm{ind}} \mathsf{RO}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{SequenceMAC}[H]
  \approx \operatorname{SequenceMAC}[\mathsf{RO}]
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{SequenceMAC}[\mathsf{RO}]
  \approx \mathsf{URF}_{\mathrm{InputSequence}}.
$$

The final statement is entirely on the CR18 law surface:
`PFunPDS`, $\Delta=$ `maxAdvantage`, and
$\lceil q\rceil=$ `PFunPDS.filterQueries`.  There is no `advPRF`, `advNPRF`,
H-technique `Adv`, game, adversary, or extra oracle-program object in the
statement.

There are two necessary qualifications.

1. `SequenceMAC[RO]` is not literally a uniform function with zero error.
   Distinct encodings give distinct *inner* RO inputs, but their independent
   RO answers can collide.  When two sequences have the same item count, such
   an inner-tag collision makes their outer RO inputs equal.  This is the
   encoding-collision term.
2. If the key or customization is longer than one hash block, C2SP also makes
   raw derivation queries $K$ or $S$.  The `SEQHSH_I`/`SEQHSH_O` headers separate
   framed inner calls from framed outer calls, but they do not by themselves
   prove that an arbitrary raw $K$ or $S$ cannot equal a framed call.  The
   general theorem therefore carries a schedule-overlap term.  It vanishes in
   the short-key/short-customization corollary, or under an explicit freshness
   premise.

The inner-tag qualification is predicted by R1's collision branches, and the
raw-call qualification by R2's long-customization overlap analysis; omitting
either would make the R3 guardrail false.

## 1. The actual objects

Fix the finite output type

$$
C := \texttt{HashOutput L}, \qquad N := |C|,
$$

a C2SP block size `b`, a customization `S`, and a probability distribution
`DK` on `SequenceMACKey`.

An important finiteness point is forced by the actual API.  RandomSystems
defines `PFunPDS.URF` by uniformly sampling the whole finite function space
$X\to Y$
([`RandomSystems/PDS.lean`, `ofFunDist` and `URF`](../../RandomSystems/PDS.lean#L117-L138)).
There is no `Fintype (List Byte)`, so the expression
`PFunPDS.URF (X := List Byte)` is not available and must not appear in the
guardrail.  The existing sponge model makes the same move: its RO domain is a
finite length-bounded message type, not an unbounded list
([`RandomSystemsCC/Sponge.lean`](../../RandomSystemsCC/Sponge.lean#L198-L214)).

Use the finite active-call domain generated by the already defined protocol
step:

$$
\mathcal X_{b,S,L}
 := \left\{x:\texttt{List Byte}\ \middle|\
 \begin{array}{l}
   \exists K:\texttt{SequenceMACKey},\;
   \exists M:\texttt{InputSequence},\;
   \exists ys:\texttt{List }C,\\
   |ys|\le 3\ \land\
   \texttt{sequenceMACStep }b\;S\;K\;M\;ys=\texttt{Sum.inl }x
 \end{array}\right\}.
$$

`SequenceMACKey`, `InputSequence`, $C$, and lists of $C$ of length at most
three are finite, so $\mathcal X_{b,S,L}$ is finite.  It contains every hash
query made in any of the four branches of `sequenceMACStep`, including raw
long-key/customization derivation calls.
Reuse the existing `InputSequence`/`HashOutput` finite instances and
`zeroHashOutput` default from
[`Finite.lean`](../SequenceHash/RandomSystems/Finite.lean#L18-L56); only the
`SequenceMACKey` and active-subtype instances are new.

Sample the actual finite RO table

$$
r\leftarrow\texttt{Dist.uniform }(\mathcal X_{b,S,L}\to C),
$$

and extend it to a `FixedHash L` by

$$
\operatorname{extend}(r)(x)=
\begin{cases}
r\langle x,h_x\rangle,&h_x:x\in\mathcal X_{b,S,L},\\
c_0,&x\notin\mathcal X_{b,S,L},
\end{cases}
$$

for a fixed $c_0:C$.  Define

$$
D_{\mathsf{RO}}
 := \texttt{Dist.fTransform extend}
      (\texttt{Dist.uniform }(\mathcal X_{b,S,L}\to C))
 : \texttt{Dist (FixedHash L)}.
$$

The hash resource before extension is exactly
`PFunPDS.URF (X := 𝓧) (Y := C)`; the extension merely gives the existing
SequenceMAC converter its current `List Byte → C` interface.  Its arbitrary
off-domain value is unobservable because every scheduled query carries a proof
of membership in $\mathcal X_{b,S,L}$.  This active-domain realization lemma is
new, but the RO itself is the existing `PFunPDS.URF` model.

Reuse the existing keyed converter law

$$
\mathsf{SM}(D)
 := \texttt{sequenceMACSystem }b\;S\;DK\;D
 : \texttt{PFunPDS InputSequence }C.
$$

It samples one key, independently samples the hash function, and applies the
resulting probabilistic converter through `PFunPDC.apply`
([`SequenceMACRealization.lean`](../SequenceHash/RandomSystems/SequenceMACRealization.lean#L109-L124),
[`ResourceView.lean`, `PFunPDC.apply`](../../RandomSystems/ResourceView.lean#L212-L225)).
The pointwise and law-level realization theorems already identify this system
with the literal C2SP `F = 1` function
([`SequenceMACRealization.lean`, `sequenceMAC_ofStep_functionEvaluator` and
`sequenceMACSystem_realization`](../SequenceHash/RandomSystems/SequenceMACRealization.lean#L126-L243)).

Write

$$
\mathsf{SM}_{\mathsf{RO}} := \mathsf{SM}(D_{\mathsf{RO}})
$$

and

$$
\mathsf{RF}
 := \texttt{PFunPDS.URF}
      \; (X:=\texttt{InputSequence})\; (Y:=C).
$$

The real construction $\mathsf{SM}_H$ is the single-interface PDS obtained by
attaching the same SequenceMAC converter to the honest hash interface of the
real resource that constructs $H$.  Section 3 spells out the required bridge
from the two-interface indifferentiability resource to this PDS.

## 2. The actual `abstract-crypto` indifferentiability API

Let `A : MetricResourceAlgebra I`, let `σ` be a resource signature, and let
`con` and `adv` be the construction and adversarial interfaces.  The fixed
protocol notion in the current API is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\texttt{A.ProtocolIndifferentiable con adv }\pi\;R\;S\;\varepsilon\\
&\quad:\!\Longleftrightarrow
\exists\,\mathsf{sim},\;
  \texttt{A.edistQ}
    (\texttt{attachQ con }\pi\;R)
    (\texttt{attachQ adv sim }S)
  \le \varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
$$

This was the literal definition in
`ConstructiveCrypto/ResourceTheory/TwoPartyShapes.lean` (historical — that file
and the whole `ResourceTheory` subtree were deleted from `abstract-crypto` on
2026-07-18).  The live declarations carrying this notion are
`AbstractCrypto.Indifferentiable` (the existential-protocol MauRen11 Def 23
form) and, for a fixed protocol, `AbstractCrypto.constructs_of_simulator`,
both in
[`AbstractCrypto/Relaxations.lean`](../../../abstract-crypto/AbstractCrypto/Relaxations.lean).
The deleted theorem
`MetricResourceAlgebra.ProtocolIndifferentiable.constructs` turned the witness
into the construction

$$
\{R\}\;\mathrel{—[\pi]→}\;
  \operatorname{eball}_{\varepsilon}
    ((\operatorname{star}\,H_{\mathrm{adv}})\{S\}),
$$

without reproving any simulator calculus; its live counterparts are
`AbstractCrypto.constructs_of_simulator` (fixed protocol) and
`AbstractCrypto.Indifferentiable.construct` (existential protocol), both in
[`AbstractCrypto/Relaxations.lean`](../../../abstract-crypto/AbstractCrypto/Relaxations.lean).

The names must not be conflated:

- there is currently no theorem named `ProtocolIndifferentiable.trans`;
- `AbstractCrypto.Indifferentiable.trans` is the generic, existential-protocol
  composition theorem.  It adds errors and requires nonexpansion plus the
  explicit commutation of the outer protocol with the inner simulator
  (live in
  [`AbstractCrypto/Relaxations.lean`](../../../abstract-crypto/AbstractCrypto/Relaxations.lean),
  `Indifferentiable.trans`; the old
  `AbstractCrypto/Specifications/Indifferentiability.lean` path no longer
  exists);
- for a fixed `ProtocolIndifferentiable` hypothesis, `.constructs` first
  exposes the simulator- and metric-relaxed construction.  The reusable
  downstream composition theorem is `Constructs.relax_trans`; when both legs
  are metric relaxations, its additive specialization is
  `Constructs.eball_trans`
  (both live in
  [`AbstractCrypto/Relaxations.lean`](../../../abstract-crypto/AbstractCrypto/Relaxations.lean);
  the old `AbstractCrypto/Specifications/` paths no longer exist).

For the single R3 context, one can equivalently open the simulator witness and
use attachment nonexpansion directly:

$$
d(\alpha R,\alpha T)\le d(R,T),
$$

which was the `edist_attach_le` field of `MetricResourceAlgebra`
(historical — `ConstructiveCrypto/ResourceTheory/Metric.lean` was deleted).
The live form is the `IsNonexpandingSMul` mixin and its `edist_smul_le`
consequence in
[`AbstractCrypto/CryptographicAlgebra.lean`](../../../abstract-crypto/AbstractCrypto/CryptographicAlgebra.lean).
The formal proof should use the construction route (`.constructs` plus the
generic relaxed-composition theorem) when R3 is chained with R5, and the direct
nonexpansion route for the numerical PDS corollary.  Neither route introduces a
SequenceHash-specific composition proof.

## 3. Composition and the faithful PDS bridge

Assume

$$
h_{\mathrm{ind}}:
\texttt{A.ProtocolIndifferentiable con adv }\pi_H\;R_H\;R_{\mathsf{RO}}
  \;(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}:\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\infty}).
$$

Opening the definition supplies a simulator `sim` and

$$
d(\pi_H R_H,\mathsf{sim}\,R_{\mathsf{RO}})
  \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}.
$$

Let $\alpha_{\mathrm{SM}}$ be the SequenceMAC converter at `con`.  Attachment
nonexpansion gives

$$
d(\alpha_{\mathrm{SM}}\pi_H R_H,
  \alpha_{\mathrm{SM}}\mathsf{sim}\,R_{\mathsf{RO}})
  \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}.
$$

Because `con ≠ adv`, resource-algebra attachment commutation moves the simulator
past SequenceMAC:

$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{SM}}\mathsf{sim}\,R_{\mathsf{RO}}
  \approx
\mathsf{sim}\,\alpha_{\mathrm{SM}}R_{\mathsf{RO}}.
$$

Finally block or forget the adversarial interface and project the honest MAC
interface to a one-interface PDS.  Another nonexpanding attachment/projection
then yields the CR18 statement

$$
\boxed{
\Delta(\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_H,
       \lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_{\mathsf{RO}})
 \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}.
}
$$

For a query-budgeted indifferentiability leaf, instantiate its resource budget
at the number of hash calls made by the SequenceMAC converter.  The existing
`sequenceMACStep` has a uniform `AnswersWithin` bound of four: short/short makes
two calls, exactly one long input makes three, and long/long makes four
([`SequenceMACRealization.lean`](../SequenceHash/RandomSystems/SequenceMACRealization.lean#L64-L107)).
Thus a generic budgeted corollary uses

$$
Q(q)=4q,
$$

while the short-key/short-customization corollary can use $Q(q)=2q$.  If
`ProtocolIndifferentiable` is assumed with one unbounded error
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}$, no budget conversion appears in the theorem.
The four-call proof is currently local to
`sequenceMAC_ofStep_functionEvaluator`; extract it as the public lemma
`sequenceMACStep_answersWithin_four` and make both the realization and R3 reuse
it.

### What is already bridged, and what is not

RandomSystems already has almost all of the metric-to-PDS endpoint:

- the CR18 metric carrier is the normalized, total subtype `ResPT` and its
  metric is inherited in `probMetricAlgebra`
  ([`RandomSystemsCC/ProbMetric.lean`](../../RandomSystemsCC/ProbMetric.lean#L627-L645));
- `edistD_eq_maxAdvantage` identifies that metric with the `ENNReal.ofReal` of
  the **symmetrized** CR18 distance
  $\max(\Delta(S,T),\Delta(T,S))$
  ([`RandomSystemsCC/VerdictBehavior.lean`](../../RandomSystemsCC/VerdictBehavior.lean#L519-L576)).
  For R3's one-directional target, use
  `ofReal_maxAdvantage_le_edistD`; do not silently identify a signed
  `maxAdvantage` with a symmetric metric.

The bridge is not directly usable for SequenceMAC today.  The present
RandomSystemsCC carrier takes converters to be memoryless same-alphabet routers
`(X → X) × (Y → Y)`
([`RandomSystemsCC/DetAlgebra.lean`](../../RandomSystemsCC/DetAlgebra.lean#L133-L141)),
whereas `sequenceMACConverter` is a type-changing, multi-round probabilistic
converter

$$
\texttt{PFunPDC InputSequence C (List Byte) C}.
$$

Moreover, the abstract `ResourceAlgebra` contract deliberately exposed
same-alphabet attachment at a fixed signature (historical —
`ConstructiveCrypto/ResourceTheory/Algebra.lean` was deleted).  The live
contract is the selected `[∀ i, Monoid (Γ i)] [MulAction (∀ i, Γ i) Φ]`
surface in
[`AbstractCrypto/CryptographicAlgebra.lean`](../../../abstract-crypto/AbstractCrypto/CryptographicAlgebra.lean),
instantiated at one fixed signature by
[`RandomSystemsCC/TypedFinite.lean`](../../RandomSystemsCC/TypedFinite.lean).
Consequently, the formal R3 proof needs one faithful adapter, not a guessed
coercion:

1. put the MAC and hash alphabets in a tagged common interface, or finish a
   full protocol-converter carrier whose attachment computes as
   `PFunPDC.apply`;
2. prove the representative computation
   `attachQ αSM = sequenceMACSystem` from the existing realization theorem;
3. provide the nonexpanding projection/blocking map from the two-interface
   resource to the one-interface PDS;
4. expose the resulting public lemma

   $$
   \texttt{protocolIndifferentiable_sequenceMAC_le}:
   \quad
   \Delta(\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_H,
          \lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_{\mathsf{RO}})
   \le (\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}:\mathbb R).
   $$

Use an `NNReal` error parameter at this endpoint and coerce it to `ENNReal` in
the indifferentiability hypothesis; this avoids an unnecessary
`ε ≠ ⊤` premise when returning to the real-valued $\Delta$ surface.

The adapter must reuse `PFunPDC.apply`, metric inheritance,
`edistD_eq_maxAdvantage`, attachment nonexpansion, and the abstract relaxed
composition theorems.  Rebuilding a special statistical-distance or simulator
proof inside SequenceHash would be a reproof.

For the filtered $Q(q)$ form, also generalize the existing deterministic
query-budgeted converter DPI
`maxAdvantage_filterQueries_applyDDC_le`
([`RandomSystems/AbsorbDPI.lean`](../../RandomSystems/AbsorbDPI.lean#L2234-L2260))
to probabilistic converters.  Its proof should condition on converter coins and
reuse the deterministic theorem; it must not be duplicated in the SequenceHash
directory.

## 4. Why `SequenceMAC[RO]` is a PRF

Fix a sampled key $K$.  For an outside query $M$, write

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_I(K,M)
  &= \operatorname{HeaderI}(b,1,K)\,\|\,K'\,\|\,
     \operatorname{encodeItems}(M),\\
z(K,M) &= R(x_I(K,M)),\\
x_O(K,S,M,z)
  &= \operatorname{HeaderO}(b,1,S,K)\,\|\,S'\,\|\,K'\,\|\\
  &\qquad \operatorname{MSBF}(|M|)\,\|\,
     \operatorname{MSBF}(L)\,\|\,z,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $R$ is the extension of a uniform random function on the finite active
domain $\mathcal X_{b,S,L}$.  Every displayed input lies in that domain by
construction.

### 4.1 Deterministic distinctness facts

For fixed $K$ and distinct $M,N$:

1. `encodeItems_injective` gives

   $$
   x_I(K,M)\ne x_I(K,N).
   $$

   This is the proved C2SP unique-parsing theorem
   ([`Encoding.lean`](../SequenceHash/Encoding.lean#L154-L162)).
2. The first eight bytes of the two headers are `SEQHSH_I` and `SEQHSH_O`.
   Hence every framed inner point is distinct from every framed outer point.
   The function indicator $F=1$ also separates SequenceMAC from C2SP functions
   using another `F`; it is not a substitute for the I/O indicator within
   SequenceMAC.
3. Fixed-width `encodeMSBF_injective` makes the outer parsing unambiguous.  In
   particular,

   $$
   x_O(K,S,M,z)=x_O(K,S,N,z')
   \Longrightarrow |M|=|N|\ \land\ z=z'.
   $$

The existing R1 theorem packages the same collision accounting for literal
SequenceHash: a distinct-input equality can only come from an outer collision,
an inner collision, or a long-customization derivation collision
([`Spec.lean`, `sequenceHash_collision_of_distinct_inputs`](../SequenceHash/Spec.lean#L98-L117));
`sequenceHash_pair_injective` is its direct sound-encoding corollary
([`SoundEncoding.lean`](../SequenceHash/SoundEncoding.lean#L18-L31)).

That theorem is specialized to empty-key, $F=2$ SequenceHash, so it must not be
misapplied verbatim to keyed $F=1$ SequenceMAC.  Following “never reprove;
generalize”, refactor its deterministic core into a public
`sequenceFunction_collision_of_distinct_inputs` parameterized by $F,K,S$, and
make the current SequenceHash theorem plus the new SequenceMAC collision lemma
corollaries.  The parsing argument is reused once.

### 4.2 The only ordinary RO collision

Under `DerivationFresh`, distinct outside queries give inner points that are
distinct from one another and from every earlier raw/framed point, so their
tags are fresh independent uniform values.  Define

$$
\mathsf{BadTag}_q
 := \{\exists i<j:\ M_i\ne M_j\ \land\
       z(K,M_i)=z(K,M_j)\}.
$$

Restricting to pairs with equal item count would be exact, because unequal
counts already separate the outer points; using every distinct pair is the
clean upper bound.  The existing collision scalar is

$$
\operatorname{pairCollisionUnionBound}(C,q)
 = \frac{|\{(i,j):i<j<q\}|}{|C|},
$$

defined in
[`RandomSystems/SwitchingLemma.lean`](../../RandomSystems/SwitchingLemma.lean#L844-L875).
The repository already proves the uniform-function fixed-query collision bound
with exactly this scalar
([`SequenceMACPRF.lean`, `uniform_fixedQuery_collision_le`](../SequenceHash/RandomSystems/SequenceMACPRF.lean#L1079-L1108))
and the generic restriction identity saying that a uniform function restricted
along an embedding is uniform
([`SwitchingLemma.lean`, `uniform_restrict`](../../RandomSystems/SwitchingLemma.lean#L1843-L1874)).

Conditioned on $\neg\mathsf{BadTag}_q$ and schedule freshness, all outer points
are distinct and disjoint from the inner points.  Lazy sampling of $R$ therefore
makes the outer answers independent uniform elements of $C$.  Repeated outside
queries are answered consistently and consume no new point.  Thus the visible
law is the $q$-query restriction of `PFunPDS.URF`.

The new proof obligation is only the adaptive scheduling wrapper: reduce the
converter's sequence of fresh inner points to the existing uniform-function
collision lemma, then use `uniform_restrict` on the good schedule.  The
probability and pair-counting facts themselves already exist and must be reused.

### 4.3 Exact RO-to-PRF lemmas

Introduce one public predicate `DerivationFresh b S DK` saying that, for every
key in `DK.support` and every possible tuple of intermediate digest values,
each raw derivation point actually queried by the C2SP schedule is disjoint
from every framed inner or outer point.  Quantifying over intermediate values
makes this a deterministic syntactic premise, not an event depending on the
sampled RO.  Repeated raw derivation points themselves are harmless; the
predicate only excludes a raw point being reinterpreted as a framed point.
Prove the short-input constructor:

$$
(|S|\le b\ \land\ \forall K\in\operatorname{supp}(DK),\ |K|\le b)
\Longrightarrow \operatorname{DerivationFresh}(b,S,DK),
$$

because no raw derivation query is made in that case.

The exact clean lemma to implement is:

```text
theorem sequenceMAC_ro_prf_le
    (q : ℕ) (b : BlockSize) (S : ByteString)
    (DK : Dist SequenceMACKey) (hDK : DK.isProbDist)
    (hfresh : DerivationFresh b S DK) :
  Δ(⌈q⌉ sequenceMACSystem b S DK (sequenceMACRODist b S),
    ⌈q⌉ PFunPDS.URF (X := InputSequence) (Y := HashOutput L))
    ≤ (pairCollisionUnionBound (HashOutput L) q : ℝ)
```

This is a pure $\Delta$ statement.  It is valid for any normalized key law;
uniformity of the hidden RO, independence of `DK` and the RO (`Dist.prod`), and
support freshness are what the proof uses.

For arbitrary long keys/customizations, define a role-separated RO system in
which raw derivation, framed inner, and framed outer calls have tagged domains,
and let

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{sched}}(q)
 := \Delta(\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_{\mathsf{RO}},
           \lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_{\mathsf{RO}}^{\mathrm{sep}}).
$$

This is a distance between two `PFunPDS` systems, not a game probability.  R1's
generalized parsing theorem supplies the within-role injectivity facts, while a
new public call-role lemma identifies the additional raw/framed overlap cases.
The general lemma is

$$
\boxed{
\Delta(\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_{\mathsf{RO}},
       \lceil q\rceil\mathsf{RF})
\le
\rho_{\mathrm{sched}}(q)
+ (\operatorname{pairCollisionUnionBound}(C,q):\mathbb R).
}
$$

Define the complete encoding-collision term by

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{enc}}(q)
 := \rho_{\mathrm{sched}}(q)
  + (\operatorname{pairCollisionUnionBound}(C,q):\mathbb R).
$$

Under `DerivationFresh`, $\rho_{\mathrm{sched}}(q)=0$.  If a looser display is
desired, reuse `pairCollisionUnionBound_le_birthday` to obtain

$$
\operatorname{pairCollisionUnionBound}(C,q)
\le \frac{q^2}{2N}
$$

([`SwitchingLemma.lean`](../../RandomSystems/SwitchingLemma.lean#L1647-L1664)).

## 5. R3 guardrail

Let $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}(Q)$ be the bound supplied by the R5
`ProtocolIndifferentiable` hypothesis when the underlying resource is restricted
to $Q$ construction-interface calls.  With $Q(q)=4q$ for the existing general
SequenceMAC schedule, the theorem to freeze is

$$
\boxed{
\Delta\!\left(
  \lceil q\rceil\operatorname{SequenceMAC}[H]_{S,DK},
  \lceil q\rceil\texttt{PFunPDS.URF}_{\mathrm{InputSequence},C}
\right)
\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}(4q)
+ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{enc}}(q).
}
$$

Expanded,

$$
\Delta(\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_H,\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{RF})
\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}(4q)
+ \rho_{\mathrm{sched}}(q)
+ (\operatorname{pairCollisionUnionBound}(C,q):\mathbb R).
$$

The proof is one triangle:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_H,\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{RF})
&\le
\Delta(\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_H,
       \lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_{\mathsf{RO}})\\
&\quad+
\Delta(\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_{\mathsf{RO}},
       \lceil q\rceil\mathsf{RF})\\
&\le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}(4q)
   + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{enc}}(q),
\end{aligned}
$$

using the already public `maxAdvantage_triangle`
([`RandomSystems/AbsorbDPI.lean`](../../RandomSystems/AbsorbDPI.lean#L1024-L1042)).

If the hypothesis is the unbudgeted

$$
\texttt{ProtocolIndifferentiable }\cdots
  (\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}:\mathbb R_{\ge0}),
$$

replace $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}(4q)$ by
$(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}:\mathbb R)$.  Under short key and short
customization, the sharper corollary is

$$
\Delta(\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{SM}_H,\lceil q\rceil\mathsf{RF})
\le
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ind}}(2q)
+ (\operatorname{pairCollisionUnionBound}(C,q):\mathbb R).
$$

The target and every public helper remain pure `PFunPDS`/$\Delta$/$\lceil
q\rceil`.  The simulator exists only inside the indifferentiability hypothesis
and its abstract composition proof; it is not exposed in the R3 conclusion.

## 7. Contrast with R2

R2 assumes the Merkle--Damgård compression family
$f:C\to B\to C$ is a PRF and proves security by the Gaži--Pietrzak--Rybár
cascade reductions.  Its bound contains the concrete run-up hop, adaptive and
nonadaptive compression-PRF terms, the cascade-depth multiplier, and the
compression birthday term.  It is MD-specific.

R3 assumes the **whole hash construction** $H$ is indifferentiable from a
random oracle in the actual resource-algebra sense.  The internal structure of
$H$ disappears after the composition step.  What remains is only the
information-theoretic analysis of C2SP SequenceMAC over a random function:
unique byte parsing, I/O/F domain separation, possible raw-derivation overlap,
and the inner-tag birthday collision.  Thus R3 is the hash-agnostic complement
to R2, not a reformulation of the compression-PRF theorem and not an
H-technique argument.
